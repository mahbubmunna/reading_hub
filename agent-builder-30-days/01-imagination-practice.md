# Imagination Practice

Imagination is not a mood. It is a set of habits: produce a lot, combine distant things, and leave room for boredom. Each habit here is small enough to survive a nine month old.

## 1. The walk with one question

Every morning, 20 to 30 minutes outside. No phone, no podcast, no music. Before leaving, write one question on a sticky note from yesterday's work. Examples:

- Why did the agent loop twice on the same tool?
- What would this look like if the user could not type?
- What is the smallest version of memory that would actually help?

Hold the question loosely. Do not force an answer. Most of your best ideas this month arrive here, usually around minute 15.

## 2. Three ideas a night

Open `templates/idea-log.md`. Write three ideas. Number them. Rules:

- No judging while writing. Bad ideas are the price of good ones.
- One line each. If it needs more, it is a project, not an idea.
- Any domain. Agents, products, your apps, the clinic, the baby, anything.

By day 30 you have 90 ideas. About five will be good. You cannot know which five in advance, which is why you write all 90.

## 3. One constraint prototype per week

Two hours maximum, on the weekend. Constraints force structure you would never choose on your own.

| Week | Constraint |
|---|---|
| 1 | An agent with no tools. It can only think and answer. How useful can it be? |
| 2 | An agent that may only ask questions. Never answers. Can it still help someone solve a problem? |
| 3 | Two agents that must disagree before a final answer is given. |
| 4 | An agent for your wife or your baby. Something real, tiny, and used. |

Write ten lines afterward: what surprised you, what would you keep.

## 4. Read outside the field

Twenty minutes, four nights a week, paper book in bed instead of the phone.

- Weeks 1 and 2: Ted Chiang, *Stories of Your Life and Others*. Read "Story of Your Life", "Understand", and "Division by Zero" first.
- Weeks 3 and 4: Douglas Hofstadter, *Gödel, Escher, Bach*, chapters 1 to 5. Slow is fine.

Why these two: both are about minds, symbols, and systems, which is exactly what an agent builder thinks about, but they come at it from directions no tech blog ever will. Write one line in the idea log after each session: one analogy to agents.

## 5. Steal from another domain

Once a week, pick something that coordinates well and ask how it handles handoffs, failure, and overload:

- A hospital triage desk
- An air traffic control tower
- A restaurant kitchen during rush
- A courtroom clerk's office
- A shipping port

Write five lines: what they do, what your agent could copy. The clinic receptionist and the causelist app both have direct analogies here.

## 6. Explain out loud

Two minutes, daily, recorded on your phone. "Today I built X. It works by Y. The part I do not understand is Z." Listen back once a week. You will hear the rambling shrink. This also rebuilds the level by level thinking you had at 23, because speaking forces order.

## 7. The embarrassment question

Once a week: "What would make this project embarrassing in two years?" Write the answer. It is usually the next idea.

## What to expect

Week 1: the walk feels pointless and the idea log feels forced. Normal.
Week 2: withdrawal from feeds. Restless, bored, tempted. Normal. Keep the blockers.
Week 3: the first idea that surprises you. Usually on the walk.
Week 4: you start thinking about the problem when you are not working. That is the old you coming back.
